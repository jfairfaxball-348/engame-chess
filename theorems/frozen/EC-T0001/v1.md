## Statement

**Scope.** Orthodox chess on the 8×8 board. Exactly one White king and one Black king, no other pieces. The side to move is part of the state. Rule edition: FIDE Laws of Chess, effective 1 January 2023, https://handbook.fide.com/chapter/E012023, Articles 1.2, 1.4, 1.4.1, 1.5, 3.1–3.10, 5.2.2.

**Theorem (prose).** Let `p = (w, b, t)` be a two-king configuration with Chebyshev distance `d∞(w,b) ≥ 2`. Then no finite series of legal moves from `p`, of any length `n ≥ 0`, with moves chosen freely for both players, reaches a position in which either colour is checkmated. That is, `p` is dead. Every two-king position that arises in a legally played game satisfies `d∞(w,b) ≥ 2`, so it is dead, and by Article 5.2.2 the game is drawn immediately.

This is a **no-possible-mate** statement. It is strictly stronger than "neither side can force mate", and it is distinct from the repetition, 50-move and 75-move rules.

**Theorem (Lean, verbatim `#check` output at the proof commit):**
```
@EndgameChess.KK.dead_of_admissible : ∀ {p : EndgameChess.KK.KKState},
  EndgameChess.KK.Admissible p → EndgameChess.KK.Dead p
EndgameChess.KK.dead_of_last_move_legal : ∀ (q : EndgameChess.KK.KKState),
  (∃ p, EndgameChess.KK.LegalKK p q) → EndgameChess.KK.Dead q
```
Here `Admissible p := 2 ≤ dInf p.wk p.bk` and `Dead p := ¬ ∃ q, Reach p q ∧ (Checkmate .white q ∨ Checkmate .black q)`. `Reach` is the reflexive–transitive closure of `LegalKK`.

## Trust boundary (what Lean does *not* check)
1. **A1:** FIDE 3.8.1 "adjoining square" means an on-board Chebyshev neighbour.
2. **Prose L3a:** the FIDE-legal moves of a two-king position are exactly `LegalKK` (no castling without a rook, no king capture by 1.4.1, the 3.9.2 self-check rule).
3. **General L0:** a game-arisen two-king position may follow a *capture* from larger material. Lean's `admissible_of_last_move_legal` covers only two-king predecessors. The general case rests on the prose proof (3.9.2 applied to the last move).
4. **The Article 5.2.2 consequence** (immediate draw) is normative and is argued in prose.
5. **The Lean kernel, the toolchain binary and the standard axioms** are trusted.
