# EC-T0002 v1 — King and bishop versus king is a dead position

**Status:** FROZEN (session S006, 2026-10-16). Source candidate: **EC-C002**.
**Proof-source commit:** `15610256c9588e2bb5e0c19b035bbfd4437b2e31`. This manifest is in a later commit and does not cite its own SHA.
**Depends on:** [EC-T0001](../EC-T0001/v1.md) (K vs K), frozen.
**Attribution:** classical. K+B vs K is a standard case of insufficient mating material. Nothing here is claimed as novel.

## Statement

**Scope.** Orthodox chess on the 8×8 board. The material is exactly king + bishop for one side (the *strong* side, either colour) against a lone king. Side to move is part of the state. Rule edition: FIDE Laws of Chess, effective 1 January 2023 (https://handbook.fide.com/chapter/E012023), Articles 1.2, 1.4, 1.4.1, 1.5, 3.1–3.2, 3.5, 3.8–3.10 and 5.2.2.

**Theorem (prose).** Let `p` be a K+B vs K configuration with all three pieces on distinct squares, in which the side not to move is not in check (`LKBK`).

Then no finite series of legal moves from `p` reaches a checkmate of either colour. This holds for series of any length `n ≥ 0`, with free move choices for both players, including the lone king capturing the bishop, after which the position is K vs K. So `p` is dead.

Every game-arisen K+B vs K position satisfies `LKBK`. Hence it is dead, and by Article 5.2.2 the game is drawn immediately.

This is a **no-possible-mate** statement, which is stronger than "no forced mate". Stalemates do occur in K+B vs K; stalemate is not checkmate.

**Key lemma (B2).** A lone king in check always has a legal move. The check must come from the bishop, so the king stands on the bishop's colour. One rank-adjacent and one file-adjacent neighbour of the king are therefore of the other colour, and the bishop cannot attack them. The enemy king cannot cover both of those squares without itself being adjacent to the lone king, which is illegal.

**Theorem (Lean, verbatim `#check` output at the proof commit):**
```
@EndgameChess.KBK.dead_of_LKBK : ∀ {p : EndgameChess.KBK.KBKState},
  EndgameChess.KBK.LKBK p → EndgameChess.KBK.Dead (EndgameChess.KBK.Pos.kbk p)
@EndgameChess.KBK.dead_of_step : ∀ {p q : EndgameChess.KBK.KBKState},
  EndgameChess.KBK.Step (EndgameChess.KBK.Pos.kbk p) (EndgameChess.KBK.Pos.kbk q) →
```

`Dead p := ¬ ∃ q, Reach p q ∧ Mated q`. `Reach` is taken over `Pos` (K+B vs K or K vs K), with the five-constructor `Step` relation. On the K vs K side it uses EC-T0001's `LegalKK` and `Checkmate`.
