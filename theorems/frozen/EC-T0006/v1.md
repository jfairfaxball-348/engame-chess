# EC-T0006 v1 — A K+R vs K position in which the lone king must take the rook (or is stalemated) is dead, in the general FIDE rules kernel

**Status:** FROZEN (session S016, 2026-10-18). Source candidate: **EC-C007**.
**Proof-source commit:** `570dfa73685ea0cc98a4ab4eaf01b5f0a1edabc4` (S015). This manifest is in a later commit and does not cite its own SHA.
**Attribution:** classical in spirit (folklore among arbiters and problemists). Nothing is claimed as novel. The contribution is a machine-checked derivation from the project's single FIDE 2023 encoding.

## Statement
**Scope.** The general rules kernel `formal/EndgameChess/Rules/`, encoding the FIDE Laws of Chess effective 1 January 2023 (Articles 1.2, 1.4.1, 2.1–3.10, 5.2.2).

**Theorem (prose).** Let `q` be a kernel position holding exactly a king and a rook of colour `σ` and a lone king of the other colour (either orientation, any flags). Suppose:
- the lone king is to move;
- `q` is not checkmate;
- every legal move of `q` captures the rook. This includes the vacuous case of no legal move at all, i.e. stalemate.

Then `q` is **dead**: no series of legal moves reaches a checkmate. If `q` has arisen, it is drawn immediately under `Rule5_2_2` (Art. 5.2.2), although a rook is on the board.

**Core lemma (material-agnostic).** A position that is not checkmate, and whose every legal successor is a two-king position, is dead.

`Rule5_2_2` is the named normative encoding of Art. 5.2.2, frozen with EC-T0003/0004. It is not a theorem.

**Definitions (Lean source, `Rules/KRK.lean`):**
```lean
def krkBoard (σ : Colour) (sk rs wk : Square) : Board := fun s =>
  if s = sk then some ⟨σ, .king⟩
  else if s = rs then some ⟨σ, .rook⟩
  else if s = wk then some ⟨σ.opp, .king⟩
  else none

def IsKRK (q : Position) (σ : Colour) (sk rs wk : Square) : Prop :=
  sk ≠ rs ∧ sk ≠ wk ∧ rs ≠ wk ∧ q.board = krkBoard σ sk rs wk
```
**Strength remark.** The proof of `krk_dead_of_forced_capture` does not use the `IsKRK` and turn hypotheses; they fix the intended reading. The underlying `dead_of_succ_KK` holds for any material.

## Non-vacuity (kernel-checked by `decide`)
- `krk_forced_capture_example`: White Kh8, Rb3, Black Ka1, White to move. **Rb3–b2 is legal.** In the result Black is not in check and `legalMoves = [Kxb2]`, which leaves K vs K. The result is proved dead *through the main theorem*.
- `krk_stalemate_dead_example`: Black Ka1, White Rb2 + Kc3, Black to move: stalemate, dead.
- `krk_live_example`: White Kh6, Ra1, Black Kh8, White to move: Ra1–a8 mates, so the position is not dead. The theorem does not cover all K+R vs K positions.

## Relation to other results
- **Depends on EC-T0003** (`dead_of_legal_into_KK`) for the K vs K successors.
- **Open companion EC-C008 (`PROPOSED`):** the converse. A K+R vs K position produced by a legal move with the strong side to move is not dead; together with EC-T0006 this would characterise the dead K+R vs K positions exactly. There is exhaustive diagnostic support (`scripts/krk_diagnostic.py`: 480 dead states per orientation, all of EC-T0006's kind, 0 mismatches) but no proof yet.
