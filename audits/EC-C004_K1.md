## 2. Attempted falsifications
1. **Castling rights after a rook capture.** `updateCastling` clears a right when its corner square is the source *or* destination, so a captured rook's right is lost. Kiwipete exercises this, and the counts agree.
2. **Castling through or out of check; b-file attacked.** See the theorems in §1.1.
3. **En passant timing and pins.** The target is set only by a two-square pawn advance and cleared on every other move. Covered by `enPassant_expired`, `enPassant_exposing_king_illegal`, and Position 3 (an en-passant pin position).
4. **Promotion.**
   - All four choices are offered (`allPromos`).
   - A non-promoting move to the last rank is rejected, and so is a promotion choice elsewhere (`promoOK`).
   - Capture-promotions are covered by Position 4/5 counts.
   - The new piece's effect is immediate: check is tested after promotion.
5. **Pins and discovered check.** The self-check test runs on the full resulting position; see `pinned_piece_cannot_move` and the mutation test.
6. **Attack vs movement.**
   - Pawn attacks are only the diagonal-forward squares.
   - A king's attack ignores self-check (3.1.3).
   - Castling attacks nothing.
   - Sliding attacks are blocked by any piece.
7. **King capture.** Rejected in `targetOK`. In legal play it is also unreachable, because the opponent's king is never left in check.
8. **Generator completeness.** Proved: `mem_legalMoves` and `mem_legalPositions`. The candidate list covers every own-piece source, every target, and every promotion option.
9. **Non-vacuity of `Legal`.** `initial_legal_count` gives 20 legal moves.
10. **Frozen modules.** `KK.lean` and `KBK.lean` are untouched (hashes in the closeout).
11. **Article numbering.** On the retrieved page promotion is 3.7.3.3–3.7.3.5, while its glossary cites 3.7.5. Recorded in the bridge; the text itself is unambiguous.
